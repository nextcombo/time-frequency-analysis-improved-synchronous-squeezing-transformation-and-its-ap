# [Time-Frequency Analysis]Improved Synchronous Squeezing Transformation and Its Application in Ground Penetrating Radar Detection Tasks [With MATLAB Code]

S Transformation is a fundamental time-frequency domain analysis method in the data processing of Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR). It can be used for target recognition, denoising, thin layer extraction, and high-resolution imaging. However, the S transform spectrum exhibits energy leakage near the instantaneous frequency, leading to incorrectly diffused frequency components into a broader range, affecting the accuracy and precision of GPR data processing. Synchronous compression is an effective method to prevent this spectral leakage. This paper proposes Synchronous GPST with Generalized Phase Shift (SS-GPST): firstly, it addresses the compatibility issue between S transform and synchronous compression strategy through phase shift; subsequently, SS-GPST achieves spectral energy concentration and resolution enhancement through generalized parameters and synchronous compression. Simulation results show that SS-GPST outperforms other methods in terms of focusing intensity, spectral resolution, as well as signal reconstruction accuracy and speed. In practical GPR tunnel detection data processing, the adaptability of SS-GPST is evaluated from three aspects: spectral energy distribution, thin layer identification, and data denoising. The results indicate that compared to other methods, SS-GPST accurately represents spectral components, has strong focusing intensity, and contains fewer interference components; its high-frequency slices can accurately detect the top and bottom interfaces of reinforced concrete slabs with a thickness of 3.0-3.5 cm; due to the less interference components in the SS-GPST spectrum, its inverse transformation can be an efficient denoising technique. SS-GPST is adaptable to different data processing objectives, providing high-resolution time-frequency spectra, and holds promise to replace S transform.
Time-frequency transformation decomposes signals into the time-frequency domain, revealing the distribution patterns and structural characteristics of each component. The most commonly used time-frequency transformations include Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT), Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT), and S Transform (ST). In GPR, rapid attenuation of high-frequency electromagnetic waves in media leads to weak effective signal energy, making S transform highly sensitive to low-amplitude signals in the high frequency domain.
For example, Li et al. utilized S-transformation to detect human targets and determine the frequency of life signals; Szymczyk et al. proposed a three-dimensional S-transformation for identifying sinkholes in geological structures; Riba et al. applied S-transformation to three-dimensional geophysical data to enhance the signal-to-noise ratio in archaeological exploration; Zhang et al. used generalized S-transformation slices to identify thin layers in lakebed sediments; Li et al. achieved high-resolution imaging of the shallow surface of the Moon with S-transformation. However, the spectral values of instantaneous frequency components in S-transformation diffuse into the band area centered around the instantaneous frequency, leading to energy leakage. This phenomenon results in the appearance of spectral energy in locations where it should not be present, thereby reducing the resolution of S-transformation, distorting the time-frequency distribution, and affecting the accuracy and precision of geophysical data processing.
To address the issue of energy leakage in time-frequency spectra, Dubechies et al. based on CWT proposed Synchronous Squeezing Transform (SST), which concentrates the scattered energy in the time-frequency spectrum onto the instantaneous frequency to improve spectral resolution. In recent years, SST has been successfully applied in multiple fields, such as paleoclimate research, electrocardiogram signal analysis, mechanical fault diagnosis, and signal denoising. In geophysics, Wang et al. applied SST to seismic data, revealing clearer river channel features and finer fault structure; Herrera et al. found that SST's frequency slices and signal reconstruction errors were superior to traditional methods, and used SST to identify body waves in microseism data with overlapping time phases; Mousavi et al. applied SST to seismic signal denoising, improving event detection and initial arrival time estimation.
Huang et al. proposed the synchronous squeezed S transform (SS-ST), which has higher resolution than SST, and can effectively identify natural gas hydrates in marine seismic data decomposition. On this basis, Wang et al. and Tao et al. based on the generalized S transform (GST) proposed the synchronous squeezed generalized S transform (SS-GST), further improving the time-frequency resolution, showing effectiveness in earthquake data processing. However, the S transform and the synchronous squeezed strategy are not completely compatible: the effectiveness of synchronous squeezing depends on the constant phase spectrum at frequencies, while the phase spectra of S transform and GST vary with frequency, failing to meet this condition. Therefore, SS-ST and SS-GST rely on the absolute value of the time-frequency spectrum as an alternative, leading to a decrease in time-frequency resolution, and reducing the accuracy and speed of signal reconstruction. For dense geophysical radar detection data that is easily disturbed by electromagnetic environments, maintaining high-resolution time-frequency spectrum and rapid signal reconstruction is crucial.
In response to the spectral energy leakage of S transform and its incompatibility with the synchronous squeezed strategy, this paper proposes the synchronous squeezed generalized phase S transform (SS-GPST). Based on S transform, SS-GPST can be regarded as a three-step method: firstly, perform phase shifting on the S transform to obtain the phase shifted S transform (PST), which retains the amplitude spectrum of S transform and has a frequency-invariant phase spectrum, ensuring accuracy through the synchronous squeezed strategy when superimposing complex frequency spectra; subsequently, introduce generalized parameters to enhance the flexibility of PST spectrum; finally, focus the time-frequency energy onto instantaneous frequencies through synchronous squeezing. The phase shift resolves the compatibility issue between S transform and synchronous squeezed strategy, while the combination of generalized parameters with synchronous squeezing improves time-frequency resolution. SS-GPST retains the practicality of S transform in geophysical radar processing, especially capable of providing high-focused, high-resolution time-frequency spectra, and its inverse transformation can also achieve precise and rapid signal reconstruction.
Conclusion: To address the spectral leakage issue of S-transform (ST) in GPR data processing, this paper proposes Synchronous Gradient Predictive S-Transform (SS-GPST), and provides its forward, inverse, and approximate inverse transformation methods. SS-GPST addresses the compatibility issues between ST and synchronous gradient by phase shift, utilizing generalized parameters and synchronous gradient to enhance the focus and resolution of time-frequency spectrum.
Test results on three-component synthetic signals show that SS-GPST outperforms other synchronous gradient methods in terms of focusing, resolution, reconstruction error, and speed. For actual GPR data, this paper presents a high-resolution time-frequency spectrum extraction scheme, and evaluates the adaptability of SS-GPST in tunnel inspection tasks from three aspects: frequency energy distribution, thin layer recognition, and denoising. The results are as follows:
Compared with other methods, SS-GPST can accurately represent spectral components, has strong focusing and fewer interference components;
The high-frequency slice of SS-GPST can accurately depict the top and bottom interfaces of reinforcement protection layers, with measured thickness of 3.0-3.5 cm;
Since the interference components in SS-GPST's spectrum are less, reconstructing GPR profiles through its inverse transformation is an efficient denoising technique.
Currently, SS-GPST has been integrated into the open-source platform GPRlab, serving as a new tool for GPR data processing, and is expected to replace traditional S-transform. The limitation of this study lies in the narrow scope of practical testing for SS-GPST, which will be verified through more cases in the future.
